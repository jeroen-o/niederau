The village

Welcome to Niederau

Niederau lies in the Wildschönau valley in Tyrol (Austria). It is the first village you reach when you drive up the mountain road from Wörgl in the Inn valley. It lies at about 828 metres in a wide, green high valley, surrounded by alpine meadows, forests and the peaks of the Kitzbühel Alps.

Together with Oberau, Auffach and Thierbach, Niederau forms the municipality of Wildschönau in the Kufstein district. The village has long been a favourite with Dutch and British guests: compact and friendly, with ski schools, guesthouses and hotels within walking distance of the lift.

Summer & winter

Two seasons, one village

In winter a manageable ski village with direct access to a large ski area; in summer a peaceful base for hikers, cyclists and families.

Skiing from the village

The Markbachjochbahn gondola takes you from Niederau up to the Markbachjoch. Wide, sunny slopes, ideal for beginners and families, with runs all the way back down to the village.

Ski Juwel

With the Ski Juwel Alpbachtal Wildschönau lift pass you can also ski the Schatzberg in Auffach and continue over the link into the Alpbach valley. The ski bus runs between the villages.

Ski school & children

Niederau has long had strong ski schools with lessons in Dutch, English and German, and nursery slopes close to the village. A great place to learn to ski.

Cross-country & winter walks

Cross-country trails through the valley and cleared winter footpaths past farms and little chapels. Snowshoeing and tobogganing are popular too: the valley has three toboggan runs, and the Lahnerköpfl run is in Niederau itself.

Après-ski

Cosy without being over the top: after a day on the slopes, settle into a mountain hut or a village inn for Glühwein, Kaiserschmarrn or a Tyrolean Gröstl.

A sunny village

Niederau sits open in the valley and gets plenty of sun – wonderful on a terrace in March, when the days grow longer and the snow is still good.

Hiking

Hundreds of kilometres of waymarked trails: from gentle valley walks to hikes along the ridges around the valley. The gondola gets you up high in a few minutes.

Kundler Klamm

One of the finest gorge walks in Tyrol: from the Wildschönau down along the wild Wildschönauer Ache to Kundl in the Inn valley.

(E-)mountain biking

Forest tracks and mountain lanes up to the alpine pastures; with an e-bike even the higher huts are within easy reach.

Alpine pastures & huts

In summer many alpine huts are open: cheese, buttermilk, speck and Kaspressknödel with views of the Hohe Tauern and the Kaiser mountains.

With children

Playgrounds, streams to dam, farm animals and short walks with a hut as the reward. The Wildschönau is made for families. On the edge of Niederau there is an open-air pool with three basins and a big water slide.

Tradition

Brass bands on the village square, church holidays and the Almabtrieb in autumn, when the decorated cattle return from the mountains.

Premium Card

With the Wildschönau Premium Card the mountain lifts are free in summer. Ask your accommodation whether it offers the card.

The Wildschönau

One valley, four villages

The Wildschönau high valley lies in the Kitzbühel Alps and is known as a sunny, snow-sure destination. Thanks in part to its friendly character, the valley won first prize from the ADAC – Germany’s largest motoring club – in the “Klein & Fein” (small and fine) category.

The Wildschönau is made up of four church villages – Niederau, Oberau, Auffach and Thierbach – plus the hamlet of Mühltal. The valley is ideal for relaxed skiing amid a magnificent mountain landscape. Always easy to reach and not far from Kufstein, it offers a varied summer programme as well as traditional winter sports.

Krautinger – the valley’s own schnapps

The Wildschönau is known for Krautinger, a strong spirit distilled from white turnips. According to tradition, the valley’s farmers were granted the right to distil it as far back as Empress Maria Theresa. Only a limited number of farms may still make it – you taste it at the farm itself.

Tyrolean cooking

  • Kaiserschmarrn – shredded pancake with icing sugar and apple sauce
  • Kaspressknödel – cheese dumplings, in soup or with salad
  • Tiroler Gröstl – fried potatoes, bacon and onion topped with an egg
  • Speckknödel and fresh alpine cheese

History

From farming valley to ski village

Niederau lies in the Wildschönau, a high valley in Tyrol (Austria). For centuries it was a remote valley of farmers and miners. The first tourists only arrived in the twentieth century – and in 1947 the valley’s winter sports era began in Niederau with the first chairlift in Tyrol.

That lift was quite a feat: engineer Sepp Hochmuth built it just after the Second World War, according to local lore using engines and drive wheels from tanks abandoned at Wörgl station. It opened on 14 January 1947, running from Niederau up to the Markbachjoch.

  1. 1193–1195
    First written mention of the Wildschönau.
  2. 16th century
    Mining: silver and copper ore is extracted on the slopes of the Gratlspitz near Thierbach.
  3. 18th century
    Empress Maria Theresa grants 51 farmers the right to distil Krautinger. Around fifteen farmers still use that right today.
  4. 1811
    The Wildschönau becomes an independent municipality.
  5. Early 20th century
    A road through the Kundler Klamm links the valley with the Inn valley; the covered wooden bridge dates from 1913/14. Today’s footpath follows that old road.
  6. 14 January 1947
    Tyrol’s first chairlift opens in Niederau, up to the Markbachjoch. The start of tourism in the valley.
  7. 1995
    An eight-seater gondola replaces the old chairlift: today’s Markbachjochbahn.
  8. 2022
    The mountain railway in Niederau celebrates its 75th anniversary.

Getting there

How to reach Niederau

By car

  1. Through Germany (Munich or Rosenheim) to the Kiefersfelden/Kufstein border crossing.
  2. In Austria follow the A12 Inntal motorway towards Innsbruck; take the Wörgl-Ost exit.
  3. In Wörgl follow the signs for Wildschönau. The mountain road climbs to Niederau in about 15 minutes.

Note: Austrian motorways require a vignette (digital or sticker). From the central Netherlands, allow around 9 to 10 hours’ driving.

By train

Wörgl Hbf station is on the main Munich – Innsbruck line and is well connected, including by night trains. From Wörgl a regional bus runs up into the Wildschönau.

By air

Nearest airports: Innsbruck (± 1 hour), Salzburg (± 1.5 hours) and Munich (± 2 hours).

Practical

Good to know

When is the best time to go?

For skiing, mid-January to mid-March is the most snow-sure period; March is especially sunny. In summer, June and September are quieter than the school holidays, with fine hiking weather.

Do I need a vignette?

Yes, for Austrian motorways (such as the A12 to Wörgl). You can buy it online from ASFINAG or at petrol stations just before the border. If you buy a digital vignette online, do so a few days in advance.

Is Niederau suitable for beginners and families?

Definitely. The wide slopes around the Markbachjoch, the nursery slopes by the village and the ski schools have long made Niederau popular with families and beginners.

Do people speak English?

The local language is German (with a strong Tyrolean dialect), but in hotels, ski schools and restaurants you will get by fine in English, and often in Dutch too.

Which emergency numbers should I know?

General emergency number 112. Mountain rescue in Austria: 140. In winter, always check the avalanche bulletin before a tour.

Where can I find current information on lifts, snow and events?

From the official Wildschönau tourist board and from Ski Juwel. See the links alongside.
