    Guides and practical tips

    Ski touring around Niederau

    Ski touring from Niederau: routes, piste touring, gear and avalanche safety.

    Ski touring around Niederau works well: the Wildschönau is known as a quiet touring area with gentle ridges and open alpine pastures, and the Alpbachtal and the Hohe Salve are also within reach. You can even start from the village, for example with the route via Norderberg towards the Markbachjoch area, or on a Thursday evening in midwinter at the Norderbergalm.

    Ski touring means walking uphill on skis with free heels and climbing skins and then skiing down. No lift is needed, but you do need knowledge of snow and avalanches. For beginners, piste touring and organised evenings are the gentlest way in. This page explains which areas around Niederau are confirmed for ski touring, what gear you need and how to use the avalanche bulletin.

    This guide is not a route description to follow blindly. If you do not know the route, go with a guide, and always read the current Tyrol avalanche bulletin (lawine.tirol.gv.at) first.

    Tips

    • Read the Tyrol avalanche bulletin (lawine.tirol.gv.at) every touring day and plan a turn-around point.
    • Carry a transceiver, probe and shovel, and practise rescue techniques beforehand.
    • As a beginner start with piste touring or an organised touring evening and stick to the piste times.
    • Respect wildlife protection zones and piste grooming times; winch cables are potentially fatal.
    • If you do not know the route, go with a mountain guide and check current conditions with the tourist office.

    Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

    What is ski touring?

    In ski touring you walk uphill on skis, with heels that can move freely and climbing skins under the skis that stop you from sliding back. At the top you remove the skins, lock the bindings for descent and ski down. You set your own pace and breaks and enjoy the silence and the views. Depending on the tour you walk in open terrain or along the edge of a piste. Expect real effort: a tour of a few hours and a few hundred to over nine hundred metres of ascent is normal. The more open the terrain, the more you need to know about snow, slope angle and weather.

    From Niederau: Norderberg and Markbachjoch

    The Wildschönau tourist board describes a ski tour that starts in Niederau itself: about 5.9 km and 800 m of ascent via Norderberg and the Lanerköpfl, with the descent on the Hochberg run. The tour is rated easy and takes about 3 hours 10 minutes. That makes it a logical first tour from the village, even without a car. Because part of the route follows or uses pistes, the rules for piste use apply. Check beforehand where you may walk and ski and when the pistes are being groomed.

    Ski touring evening at the Norderbergalm

    The Norderbergalm, in the ski area around the Markbachjoch, hosts a ski touring evening in winter. In the 2026 season it was scheduled on Thursday evenings from mid January to mid March, from 18:00. You walk up the forest road to the hut and relax there with regional food and drinks. Important: stick strictly to the stated times. After that the pistes are groomed with winch cables, which is potentially fatal. Ascent and descent are only allowed on the marked pistes. Dates and conditions can change each winter, so check the current programme.

    Schatzberg near Auffach

    The Schatzberg (1,898 m) near Auffach, the highest village of the Wildschönau, is known as an easy piste tour. You walk up to the left of the piste to the Schatzbergalm; the summit lies roughly 100 m of ascent above it. Tour portals quote about 900 m of ascent and a little over two hours going up. A combination with the Hahnkopf (1,902 m) is also described. Auffach is about 20 minutes by car from Niederau. Piste touring is subject to the lift operator's rules, such as grooming times and closed sections.

    Wiedersberger Horn and Reither Kogel in the Alpbachtal

    In the Alpbachtal, tour portals describe the Wiedersberger Horn (2,127 m) as a ski tour, for example from the Greiter Graben via the Moserbaumgartenalm, with about 1,000 m of ascent and roughly 2.5 to 3 hours of walking. The summit is open and offers views of the Zillertal, the Alpbachtal and the Inn valley. Another option is piste touring on the Reither Kogel (1,110 m) near Reith, on a floodlit family piste, where a ski touring evening is offered on Tuesdays. From Niederau allow about 40 minutes by car; ask about the current situation.

    Hohe Salve near Hopfgarten

    The Hohe Salve (1,828 m) near Hopfgarten in the Brixental is a well-known piste tour. A tour portal rates it easy, with about 1,200 m of ascent and around 2.5 hours going up. The route partly follows the pistes and is usually well trodden when there is enough snow. In spring the south slope beside the piste can have little snow. The region publishes rules and overview maps of wildlife protection zones for ski tourers in the Brixental and around the Hohe Salve; consult them beforehand. Hopfgarten is about 35 minutes by car from Niederau. Ask about the current piste grooming times.

    Gear and avalanche safety

    Besides touring skis, climbing skins, boots with a walk mode, poles and warm clothing, touring in open terrain requires the avalanche emergency kit: a transceiver (beacon), probe and shovel. The Austrian mountain rescue service also lists a phone, first-aid kit, bivouac sack and cold-weather clothing; a helmet and, if possible, an avalanche airbag are extras. The gear only helps if you can use it, so practise. Before every tour read the Tyrol avalanche bulletin (lawine.tirol.gv.at), plan an alternative and a turn-around point, and go in a small group. This guide does not replace that bulletin.

    Season, beginners and guidance

    The touring season follows the snow and usually runs from winter into spring; exactly how and when depends on the weather and the avalanche situation, and snow cannot be guaranteed. Beginners do best with piste touring or an organised evening such as at the Norderbergalm or the Reither Kogel. A tour with a mountain guide or ski touring instructor is a good way to learn the basics; ask the tourist office or your accommodation which guidance is available in your period. A stop at a mountain hut fits well at the end of a touring day, but plan your descent before dark.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can you go ski touring from Niederau?

    Yes. The tourist board describes an easy tour that starts in Niederau, via Norderberg and the Lanerköpfl. In the Wildschönau there is also the Schatzberg near Auffach, and in the season a ski touring evening at the Norderbergalm on Thursdays. Always read the avalanche bulletin.

    What is piste touring and is it suitable for beginners?

    In piste touring you walk up along the edge of a groomed piste and ski back down it. This is the gentlest way in because the terrain is easy to read. You must follow the lift operator's rules, such as grooming times and closed sections.

    What gear do I need?

    Touring skis, climbing skins, suitable boots and clothing, plus a transceiver, probe and shovel in open terrain. A phone, first-aid kit, bivouac sack and helmet are recommended. It is important that you can use the emergency kit.

    Are there guides or guided tours?

    Mountain guides are generally available in Tyrol. For Niederau we could not confirm a fixed provider, so ask the Wildschönau tourist office or your accommodation who leads ski tours in your period.
