    Hiking the Gratlspitz from Thierbach, Wildschönau

    A steep mountain hike up the Gratlspitz (about 1,895 m) from Thierbach or the Holzalm.

    The Gratlspitz is one of the best-known summits for hiking in the Wildschönau. It rises above Thierbach at about 1,895 m (sources give 1,894 and 1,899 m) and is roughly a 10 to 15 minute drive from Niederau. The way to the top is steep and stony; a short route from the Holzalm is about 4.4 km and is rated difficult, so this is not a walk for small children.

    For a bigger day out there is a long circuit from Thierbach, which is also described as a trail-running route and as an e-bike and mountain bike tour. We found no confirmed route details for ski touring on the Gratlspitz, so we do not describe it; in winter ask the tourist office or a mountain guide about the options.

    Below you will find what to expect, how to get there and how to stay safe.

    Tips

    • Choose the short route from the Holzalm if you have less time, but do not underestimate the steepness.
    • Go only in stable, dry weather; wet grass and stones are slippery.
    • Bring food and drink; open huts are not guaranteed.
    • With small children choose an easier outing, such as the Schatzberg circular walk.
    • For winter touring or snowshoeing, read the Tyrol avalanche bulletin and get local advice.

    Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

    About the mountain

    The Gratlspitz rises above Thierbach, a village in the Wildschönau valley. Sources give its height as 1,894 or 1,899 m, so count on about 1,895 m. There is a summit cross with a bench on top. The mountain is mainly a summer goal for experienced walkers: the paths are steep, in places exposed and sometimes secured with ropes. From Niederau, Thierbach is about 10 to 15 minutes by car, depending on where you start.

    Summer: hiking

    The shortest route starts at the Holzalm (about 1,450 m) and is about 4.4 km; the climb to the summit is steep and rated difficult by Wildschönau tourism. A longer tour starts in Thierbach, passes the Holzalmkapelle (about 1,277 m) and the Holzalm, and the return can go via the Hösljoch and the Gernalm. From the Hösljoch a marked path leads past the Halsbergalm to the top. Sure-footedness and a head for heights are needed; pick the route that matches your level and check the weather first.

    Summer: loop, trail running and cycling

    From the church in Thierbach a circuit of about 21 km is described, with roughly 1,400 m of ascent and descent and a high point of about 1,894 m. It starts on a gravel track and then follows paths towards the Holzalm; the section to the summit is partly exposed and secured with ropes. The same area is also described as an e-bike and mountain bike loop around the Gratlspitze. It is a long day for people with good fitness; allow plenty of time and bring enough water.

    Huts and refreshments

    On the way there are alpine huts and farms, including the Holzalm (with a restaurant), the Halsbergalm and the Gernalm. What is served and when they are open varies by season; ask beforehand and do not count on something always being open along the way. So bring your own drinks and lunch. A break at the Holzalm fits both the short and the long variant.

    Winter

    We found no confirmed information on ski touring or snowshoeing on the Gratlspitz itself and therefore do not describe a route. In winter the steep upper slope is a serious avalanche matter. If you want to go into the mountains on touring skis or snowshoes, consult the Tyrol avalanche bulletin (lawine.tirol.gv.at), ask locally or book a mountain guide. Simpler winter tours, such as the Feldalphorn from Schwarzenau, are covered in our other guide.

    Getting there

    From Niederau you drive to Thierbach, about 10 to 15 minutes (approximate). For the short route from the Holzalm or the Klinglerhof you need a different starting point; check the Wildschönau tourism map beforehand for where to park. The circuit starts at the church in Thierbach. Public transport through the valley reaches Thierbach; check the timetable. Start early: the day is longer than you think.

    Safety

    The Gratlspitz is not a family stroll. The climb is steep, partly exposed and secured with ropes; you need sure-footedness, good mountain boots and good fitness. Do not go in thunderstorms, rain or on wet grass. Bring water, a jacket and a phone and tell someone where you are going. This guide does not replace local knowledge or a mountain guide and is not a route description to follow blindly. In winter: Tyrol avalanche bulletin.

    Frequently asked questions

    How high is the Gratlspitz?

    About 1,895 m; sources give 1,894 and 1,899 m.

    Is the Gratlspitz difficult?

    The short route from the Holzalm is rated difficult: steep, partly exposed and secured with ropes. Sure-footedness is needed.

    Can you ski-tour the Gratlspitz?

    We found no confirmed information on this. Ask locally and consult the Tyrol avalanche bulletin.
