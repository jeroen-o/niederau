Stams Abbey

A Cistercian abbey from 1273 in the Inn Valley

Stams Abbey is a Cistercian monastery founded in 1273 by Count Meinhard II of Görz-Tyrol and his wife Elisabeth of Bavaria. The abbey church was raised to the status of basilica minor in 1983. The building contains a princes' crypt, a richly decorated pulpit, vault and wall paintings and the well-known Tree of Life altar.

According to tourist information, guided tours run on most days from June to September. Events are held as well; concerts could not be confirmed, so ask for the current programme if that is why you are going. It is a quiet cultural stop in the Inn Valley, suited to teenagers and adults who enjoy art and history.

  • AgeSuited to teenagers and adults
  • DurationApprox. 1.5 to 2 hours
  • CostPrices vary; check in advance
  • DistanceApprox. 1 hour 10 min by car
  • SeasonAll year; guided tours mainly June–September
  • LocationStams, Upper Inn Valley

Tips

  • Check guided-tour times beforehand; events can limit them in summer.
  • Dress appropriately; it is a working monastery.
  • Ask for the current concert programme if you want to hear music.
  • Combine with a visit to Innsbruck or Hall in Tirol.

Practical

Stams is in the Upper Inn Valley, roughly 1 hour 10 minutes by car from Niederau, via the A12 towards Innsbruck and further west (exit Stams/Mötz).

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.
