Tyrolean Folk Art Museum, Innsbruck

Painted chests, nativity scenes and costumes beside the Hofkirche

The Tyrolean Folk Art Museum is housed in a former Franciscan monastery right next to the Hofkirche in central Innsbruck. Its collection shows how people in Tyrol lived and celebrated over the centuries: painted chests and cupboards, tools, masks, costumes and nativity scenes.

The colourful rooms combine well with a stroll through the old town. Children mostly enjoy hunting for details, such as the masks and the tiny figures in the nativity scenes. Allow a good hour, longer if you want to take your time over the cribs.

Place: Innsbruck

  • AgeFrom approx. 6 years
  • DurationApprox. 1 to 1.5 hours
  • CostAdmission charged; see the official website for current prices
  • DistanceApprox. 1 hour by car
  • SeasonAll year (check opening hours)
  • LocationInnsbruck (old town)

Tips

  • Look out for the painted chests and cupboards; the details differ from room to room.
  • Let children pick a few masks or figures to draw later.
  • Combine the museum with the Hofkirche and the old town, both close by.

Practical

Innsbruck is about 1 hour by car from Niederau via the A12. Park in a city-centre car park, or take the train from Wörgl; the museum is within walking distance of the old town.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Tyrolean Folk Art Museum?

On Universitätsstraße, beside the Hofkirche in central Innsbruck, in a former Franciscan monastery.
