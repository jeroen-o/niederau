Rafting on the Ötztaler Ache

Whitewater rafting for experienced paddlers in the Ötztal

The Ötztaler Ache is regarded as one of the most demanding rafting rivers in Europe. The stretch near Sautens, at the entrance to the Ötztal, is about 14 km long and has class IV to V rapids. That makes the trip better suited to sporty adults than to families with children.

Trips are run by specialist operators with a guide and take roughly half a day. A minimum age of about 16 is usual on this demanding route. If you want to go on the water with younger children, look at gentler trips on other rivers, such as the Imster Schlucht (Inn), where family tours exist from about 10 years.

  • AgeAbout 16 and older (check with the operator)
  • DurationAbout half a day
  • CostPrice varies by operator; check beforehand
  • DistanceApprox. 1.5 hours by car
  • SeasonSummer (depending on water levels)
  • LocationSautens, Ötztal

Tips

  • Book ahead and ask about the minimum age and difficulty of the trip.
  • Bring a towel and dry clothes; the water is cold.
  • Wetsuit and helmet are normally supplied by the operator; ask about it.

Practical

From Niederau it is about 1.5 hours by car via the A12 towards Innsbruck and on to Sautens (Ötztal). Booking in advance is required; the starting point depends on the operator.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

Is rafting on the Ötztaler Ache suitable for children?

Usually not: the route is demanding and operators set a minimum age of about 16. For families there are gentler trips, such as on the Imster Schlucht from about 10 years.
