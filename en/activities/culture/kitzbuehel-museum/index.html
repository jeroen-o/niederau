Museum Kitzbühel

Town history, mining and the Alfons Walde collection

Museum Kitzbühel | Alfons Walde Collection sits on Hinterstadt in the centre of town, in two of its oldest buildings. A former granary and a tower of the medieval town fortifications have been incorporated into the museum. The exhibition tells the story of the region, from Bronze Age mining to the growth of Kitzbühel as a winter sports resort.

The permanent collection is devoted to the painter Alfons Walde (1891-1958): around sixty paintings and more than a hundred drawings, photographs and graphic designs. Walde was among the first to make skiing a subject of painting. The museum is easy to take in and combines well with a walk through the old town; allow one to two hours.

Place: Kitzbühel

  • AgeAll ages
  • DurationApprox. 1 to 2 hours
  • CostAdmission charged; check current prices

Tips

  • In the Walde collection, look out for the winter landscapes and skiing scenes.
  • Combine the museum with a walk through Kitzbühel's old town.
  • It also suits a rainy day.
  • Check opening hours and current exhibitions beforehand.

Practical

Kitzbühel is about 40 to 45 minutes by car from Niederau. Park in a car park near the centre; Hinterstadt lies in the pedestrian area of the old town.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

What is there to see at Museum Kitzbühel?

Regional history from Bronze Age mining to winter sports, plus the Alfons Walde collection with around sixty paintings and over a hundred drawings, photographs and designs.
