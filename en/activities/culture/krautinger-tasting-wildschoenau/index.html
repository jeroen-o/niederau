Krautinger tasting in the Wildschönau

The regional spirit made from turnips and its story

The Wildschönauer Krautinger is a protected regional spirit from Tyrol, distilled from turnips. According to tradition, Empress Maria Theresa granted 51 farmers in the valley the right to distil it in the 18th century. Around 15 farmers still use that right today.

The turnips are sliced and pressed, the juice is boiled down to a third and fermented, and then distilled twice. Strength lies between 38 and 48 per cent. The flavour is distinctive and strongly reminiscent of vegetables. On farm-to-farm walks (Hof-zu-Hof) you learn how distilling works and can taste it. Ask the tourist office which farms welcome visitors.

  • AgeTasting for adults only
  • DurationVaries by provider
  • CostPrices vary; ask in advance
  • DistanceAbout 5 to 20 min by car
  • SeasonMainly summer; offer varies
  • LocationWildschönau

Tips

  • Book a farm visit or tasting ahead; not every farmer receives visitors.
  • Taste in moderation: the spirit is strong and the flavour is not for everyone.
  • You can buy a small bottle as a local souvenir, but ask what is possible first.
  • Drivers and children can watch and listen; tasting is for adults.

Practical

The offer is spread across farms throughout the Wildschönau, about 5 to 20 min by car from Niederau. Ask about the current offer and meeting points.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

What is Krautinger made from?

From turnips. The juice is reduced, fermented and distilled twice.

How strong is Krautinger?

Between 38 and 48 per cent ABV.
