    Culture in the Wildschönau and surroundings · Summer

    Kitzbühel old town

    Car-free streets with oriels and Gothic gables in Tyrol

    Kitzbühel is best known for skiing and the Hahnenkamm race, but in summer it is also a pleasant town for a stroll. The town received its town rights in 1271 from Duke Louis II of Bavaria; silver and copper mining brought prosperity in the 16th and 17th centuries. The two main streets, Vorderstadt and Hinterstadt, are pedestrian areas. The houses have oriels and Gothic gables, and the shops range from international brands to local specialities.

    The two churches stand side by side and form the historic core. The Liebfrauenkirche is a two-storey Gothic building from the 14th century with a Baroque onion dome added in the 17th. The parish church of St Andrew is a late Gothic hall church built between 1435 and 1506, with a Romanesque tower surviving from the first church.

    A loop through the old town takes about an hour and a half, more if you sit down somewhere. The town has a different feel from the villages in the Brixental: smarter and busier, with many terraces. It suits half a day and combines well with a cable car ride or walk on the Kitzbüheler Horn.

    Tips

    • Look at the oriels and gables on the Vorderstadt and Hinterstadt.
    • Plan a coffee stop on a terrace.
    • Combine with a trip to the Kitzbüheler Horn.
    • Step inside the Liebfrauenkirche and the parish church, which stand right next to each other.
    • Come on a weekday or early in the day if you prefer it quieter.

    Practical

    Kitzbühel is roughly 45 minutes by car from Niederau, via Wörgl and the B170 through the Brixental. Much of the centre is car-free, so park on the edge or in a nearby car park and walk the rest. The centre is compact and easy to walk; allow half a day if you also want to eat or shop. On busy summer days allow for queues at the car parks.

    Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Kitzbühel worth visiting in summer?

    Yes, the old town with its pedestrian streets, churches, shops and terraces is a pleasant stop even outside the ski season.

    Since when has Kitzbühel been a town?

    Since 1271, when Duke Louis II of Bavaria granted the settlement town rights.

    Which churches are in the centre?

    The Gothic Liebfrauenkirche and the late Gothic parish church of St Andrew, side by side.
