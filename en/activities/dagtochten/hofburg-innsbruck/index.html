Hofburg Innsbruck

Imperial palace with state rooms from the era of Maria Theresa

The Hofburg at the edge of Innsbruck's old town was a Habsburg residence. In the 18th century Empress Maria Theresa had it rebuilt into a late-Baroque, rococo-style residence; that style can still be seen today.

Visitors walk through the state rooms and the imperial apartments. Most striking is the Giant Hall (Riesensaal), with portraits of Maria Theresa, her husband and their children. For children it is mainly a storytelling museum about court life; allow about an hour and a half. The palace is accessible, and the Hofgarten opposite is handy for a break.

Place: Innsbruck

  • AgeMost fun from about 6 years
  • DurationAbout 1.5 hours
  • CostAdmission varies; children pay less, check beforehand
  • DistanceAbout 50 to 60 min by car
  • SeasonAll year
  • LocationInnsbruck, Tyrol

Tips

  • Ask at the ticket desk about an audio guide or a children's trail.
  • Combine with the nearby Goldenes Dachl and the old town.
  • Take a break in the Hofgarten after your visit.
  • Check opening times beforehand; the museum is reported to be open daily, but hours vary.

Practical

The Hofburg is in central Innsbruck, about 50 to 60 minutes' drive from Niederau. Parking is possible in a nearby car park; from the station it is around 15 minutes on foot or a short bus ride.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Hofburg visit take?

Allow about an hour and a half for the state rooms and apartments.
