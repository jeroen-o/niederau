Berchtesgaden and Königssee

Day trip to Germany's Berchtesgadener Land with an alpine lake and salt mine

The Berchtesgadener Land lies just across the German border, south-east of Salzburg, and is one of the best-known areas of the Bavarian Alps. The Königssee is a narrow, deep mountain lake where only electric boats operate; the trip goes to the chapel of St. Bartholomä, a well-known spot on the shore.

Other options are the salt mine, where visitors put on miners' clothing and go underground, and the Kehlsteinhaus, reached by a special bus and a lift. It is a long day, so start early and choose two, at most three, parts; in high season expect crowds and queues.

Place: Kufstein

  • AgeAll ages
  • DurationFull day
  • CostBoat fares and admissions vary; check beforehand
  • DistanceAbout 1.5 to 2 hours by car
  • SeasonMainly spring to autumn; some parts all year
  • LocationBerchtesgaden, Bavaria (Germany)

Tips

  • Leave early; parking and boat tickets fill up quickly in high season.
  • Check opening times on the operators' websites beforehand, as the Kehlstein road is seasonal.
  • Bring a warm jacket; it is cool on the water and in the salt mine.
  • If you route over German territory, check current rules on tolls and border traffic.

Practical

From Niederau, Berchtesgaden is about 1.5 to 2 hours by car, depending on traffic and border queues. Drive early, park near the Königssee landing stage and book ahead where possible.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

Can you take a motorboat on the Königssee?

No, only electric boats of the local operator run on the lake.
