Hiking at the Wilder Kaiser

Family-friendly routes beneath the rock walls of the Kaisergebirge

The Kaisergebirge, or Wilder Kaiser, is one of Tyrol's best-known limestone massifs and is visible from far away in the Inn Valley. Near villages such as Ellmau and Going, marked routes run across meadows and through forest, with the steep rock walls right above you.

For families there are flat, quiet routes past farms and mountain inns where you can take a break. If you want to go higher, a cable car can save part of the climb. How long you are out depends on the route you choose; allow half a day to a full day. Ask the tourist office in Ellmau or Going for a map and current routes.

Place: Ellmau

  • AgeAll ages (choose the route)
  • DurationHalf to full day
  • CostWalking is free; cable car optional and charged
  • DistanceApprox. 40 min drive
  • SeasonSummer
  • LocationEllmau/Going, Wilder Kaiser (Tyrol)

Tips

  • Pick a route to suit the children's stamina; flat paths are mostly at the bottom.
  • Bring water and a picnic, although there are often huts along the way.
  • Mornings often give the best light on the rock walls.

Practical

Ellmau and Going are about 40 minutes by car from Niederau, towards St. Johann in Tirol. There is parking at the starting points.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

Are there easy walks at the Wilder Kaiser?

Yes, around Ellmau and Going there are flat marked routes across meadows and through forest; ask the tourist office for a map.
