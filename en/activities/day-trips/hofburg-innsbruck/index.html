Hofburg Innsbruck

Imperial palace with state rooms from the era of Maria Theresa

The Hofburg at the eastern edge of Innsbruck's old town was a Habsburg residence. In the 15th century Archduke Sigmund the Wealthy had a castle built here, which Emperor Maximilian I then extended. In the 18th century Empress Maria Theresa had the building rebuilt in Viennese rococo style; that style can still be seen today.

Visitors walk through the state rooms and the imperial apartments, which were restored not long ago. Most striking is the Giant Hall (Riesensaal), more than 31 metres long, with life-size portraits of Maria Theresa, her husband and their children. Since 2019 there has also been a permanent exhibition about Emperor Maximilian I. The museum is also a good choice in the rain, as you stay indoors for most of it. Ask beforehand about access if you are coming with a pushchair. For younger children a short tour is often enough.

For children it is mainly a storytelling museum about court life; allow about an hour and a half. The building is accessible to wheelchair users. The Hofgarten lies opposite, handy for a break.

Place: Innsbruck

  • AgeMost fun from about 6 years
  • DurationAbout 1.5 hours
  • CostAdmission varies; children pay less, check beforehand
  • DistanceAbout 50 to 60 min by car

  • LocationInnsbruck, Tyrol

Tips

  • Ask at the ticket desk about an audio guide or a children's trail.
  • Combine with the nearby Goldenes Dachl and the old town.
  • Take a break in the Hofgarten after your visit.
  • Check opening times and prices beforehand.
  • Look into the Maximilian I exhibition if you want to learn more of the history.

Practical

The Hofburg is in central Innsbruck, about 50 to 60 minutes' drive from Niederau via the A12. Park in a nearby car park or take the train from Wörgl; check opening times beforehand. From the Hofburg it is a few minutes' walk to the Goldenes Dachl and the rest of the old town. Bear in mind that the city can be busy in summer; setting off early helps.

Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Hofburg visit take?

Allow about an hour and a half for the state rooms and apartments.

What is the Riesensaal?

A hall more than 31 metres long with portraits of Maria Theresa, her husband and their children.

Who had the Hofburg rebuilt in rococo style?

Empress Maria Theresa, in the 18th century.
