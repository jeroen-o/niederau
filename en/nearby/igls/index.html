    Igls

    Olympic village above Innsbruck with the Patscherkofel as its home mountain.

    Igls sits on a plateau south of Innsbruck, about 5 km from the city centre at roughly 870 m. Now a district of Innsbruck, it lies at the foot of the Patscherkofel and makes a quiet base with mountain views.

    Igls became internationally known through the 1964 and 1976 Winter Olympics. The artificial-ice track for bobsleigh and luge was here, and the men's Olympic downhill was held on the Patscherkofel. The Patscherkofelbahn cable car takes visitors up to the summit at 2,246 m, with walking and cycling routes in summer and ski runs in winter.

    For families staying in Niederau, Igls is a nice mix of Innsbruck and mountains. Visit the city, then ride up by cable car, or take a winter walk along the bobsleigh track. The Tiroler Landesmuseum is in Innsbruck itself, not in Igls. Check current cable car operating times beforehand. Allow enough time, bring warm clothing and a packed lunch, and check the weather forecast beforehand so the trip is worthwhile for everyone.

    What to see and do

    • Ride the Patscherkofelbahn up to the summit at 2,246 m.
    • Olympic bobsleigh and luge track from 1964 and 1976, which you can see on a walk.
    • Winter walk along the bobsleigh track, for example to a hut on the way.
    • Mountain bike and walking routes around the Patscherkofel in summer.
    • Skiing on the Patscherkofel, Innsbruck's home mountain, in winter.

    From Niederau

    From Niederau about 1 hour 10 minutes by car, via Wörgl and the Inn valley motorway towards Innsbruck.

    Always check times, prices and opening dates with the provider beforehand; they can change.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is Olympic about Igls?

    For the 1964 and 1976 Winter Games the bobsleigh and luge track was here, and the men's downhill was on the Patscherkofel.

    How high does the Patscherkofelbahn go?

    The cable car starts at the edge of the village and ends on the Patscherkofel at 2,246 m.
