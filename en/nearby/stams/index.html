Inn valley

Stams

The Cistercian abbey of Stift Stams in the Upper Inn Valley

Stams lies at about 672 m in the Upper Inn Valley, south of the Inn, between Telfs and Innsbruck. The village is known above all for Stift Stams, a Cistercian abbey founded in 1273 by Meinhard II of Görz-Tyrol and his wife Elisabeth of Bavaria.

The abbey church, consecrated in 1284, was remodelled in high Baroque style between 1729 and 1733, with rich stucco and a large high altar in the form of a tree of life with 84 sculptures. The Fürstengruft holds members of the Tyrolean ruling house. After a long renovation, a new museum opened in November 2017, with altars, vestments, furniture, paintings and a monastery pharmacy.

The Gothic parish church and the Stamser Steg, a suspension bridge over the Inn from 1935, are also worth seeing. For families Stams is a quiet cultural stop, easy to combine with Telfs or Seefeld; check tours and opening times in advance. A visit usually takes one to two hours. The village itself is quiet; combine it with a walk along the Inn or lunch in Telfs.

Frequently asked questions

When was Stift Stams founded?

In 1273, by Meinhard II of Görz-Tyrol and his wife Elisabeth of Bavaria, as a burial place for the Tyrolean princes.

Is there a museum in Stift Stams?

Yes, the renewed museum rooms opened in November 2017. Check opening times and tours in advance.
